# Curriculum — audit, 2026-08-01

Scope: Godot practice, and why the game "works but is not fun." The test gate is
green at the time of writing — `tools/check.sh`: 24 suites, 3436 checks, 0 failures.
Nothing here is a broken build; it is all design and structure.

Findings are ordered by how much they cost the player experience. Every gameplay
claim below is measured, not asserted; the method is at the bottom.

---

## The measurement

`tools/policy_probe.gd` (added for this audit) plays 30 headless runs under four
policies and reports what each scores. If a policy that throws away a real resource
scores the same as playing properly, that resource is not a decision.

| Policy | Wins | Courses/run | Mean score | Grades |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **greedy** — play every affordable card | 3/30 | 11.2 | **76.3** | 21 S, 222 A, 88 B, 4 C, 41 F |
| **onecard** — one card per turn, waste the rest of the mana | 0/30 | 1.0 | 51.3 | 2 A, 27 B, 2 C, 60 F |
| **noward** — never play Block, healing or retain | **5/30** | 11.4 | 69.8 | 1 S, 156 A, 143 B, 43 C, 38 F |
| **onlyward** — only ever defend | 0/30 | 0.0 | 42.3 | 60 F |

Per-term averages, out of 25 each:

| Policy | Efficiency | Survival | Learning | Discovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| greedy | 23.5 (maxed in 82% of battles) | 15.9 | 16.3 (maxed 22%) | 20.6 |
| noward | 24.8 (maxed 97%) | **15.8** | 12.5 | 16.7 |

Two things fall out of this table, and they are findings 1 and 2.

---

## 1. Defence is free, so there is no defensive decision

Never playing a single Block, heal, or retain card across an entire run costs
**0.1 points of Survival** (15.9 → 15.8) and *wins more runs* (5 vs 3). The whole
Ward school — a fifth of the card pool, and the school the starting deck is 40%
built from — is close to optional. Racing is simply better than defending.

Three causes, compounding:

### 1a. Run HP is tracked, saved, and never actually used — the headline bug

`Battle.gd:41` hardcodes the player:

```gdscript
player = Combatant.new("Student", 60, 3)
```

Meanwhile `Run.hp` is written by `Run.record_result`, round-tripped through
`SaveGame`, and displayed — but **never read back into a battle**. Every fight
starts the player at a fresh 60/60 regardless of what the last fight cost.

There is therefore no attrition, which is the central resource of the genre. HP is
not a currency you spend across a run; it is a per-battle buffer that resets. That
makes healing (Study Break → Tenure, the Ward line's whole payoff) literally
incapable of mattering, and it removes the reason to ever end a fight above 1 HP.

**This is a small diff with two coupled consequences that must be fixed together:**

- `Run.record_result` sets `hp = max_hp` on an F. Once HP carries between battles,
  deliberately failing an exam becomes a free full heal — a rest site you get paid
  to visit. The strike system would start rewarding failure.
- `Main.gd:128` passes `"hp_start": run.max_hp` into `Grading.score`. If HP carries
  and `hp_start` stays `max_hp`, Survival silently becomes "fraction of *max* HP
  remaining", so damage taken three courses ago permanently caps every future grade.
  The fix must pass the battle's *actual* starting HP so Survival keeps measuring
  damage taken *in this battle*.

Fixing 1a alone is the single highest-leverage change in this document.

### 1b. Nothing telegraphs the examiner's turn

The spec is unusually emphatic here (§3): "Examiners play from decks too… Its
'intent' is simply the card it drew, shown face-up above it. **That last point is
the load-bearing one.**"

The implementation removed it. `Battle.gd:8-13` documents the removal as deliberate,
and commit `2d73488` ("make examiners a real threat") explains why: examiners were
unloseable, so the examiner was changed to spend its *full* mana each turn, and the
telegraph was deleted in the same change.

The difficulty gain in that commit came from the mana change — one action per turn
to three. Deleting the telegraph contributed no difficulty; it converted every
defensive decision into a coin flip. You cannot decide whether 10 Block is enough
when you have no idea what is landing. Combined with 1a (Block does not protect
anything that persists), the rational play is to ignore defence entirely — which is
exactly what the `noward` row measures.

Restoring the telegraph while *keeping* full-mana examiners recovers the spec's
design without giving back the difficulty. It is more work than before, because the
examiner now plays a sequence rather than one card, so the UI has to show a plan of
2–3 cards; showing only the first card of the sequence would be worse than nothing.

### 1c. Block expires before it can be tested cheaply

`Combatant.expire_block()` is correct per spec, but with 1a and 1b in place the
player is asked to pay mana for a resource that (i) protects HP that resets anyway
and (ii) is sized against an unknown threat. All three have to move together.

## 2. Grading saturates: par is a floor, not a target

`Grading.score` clamps three of its four terms at par:

```gdscript
efficiency = 25 * clampf(par_turns / turns_taken, 0.0, 1.0)
learning   = 25 * clampf(xp_banked / xp_par,      0.0, 1.0)
```

Because of the clamp there is **no gradient above par**. Winning in 5 turns against
`par_turns = 5` scores exactly the same 25 as winning in 2. Measured: Efficiency is
maxed in 82% of greedy battles (97% under `noward`), averaging 23.5/25 — it is
effectively a constant, not a discriminator. Survival is inflated by finding 1a.

A merely competent win therefore floors around 85 → **A**. Across 376 battles the
greedy bot took 243 grades of A or better and exactly 4 C's. There is no headroom
for playing well and no meaningful penalty band below it: the grade compresses
almost every outcome into one letter.

This is *the same finding* as "no meaningful decisions", seen from the scoring side.
Greedy is close to optimal play in this game — there is no targeting, no card
ordering leverage, no cost/benefit tension — so an A-heavy distribution from a bot
with no strategy is evidence that skill does not discriminate.

Worth noting the `onecard` row as the control: throwing away two thirds of your mana
*does* collapse the score (76.3 → 51.3). So the game does punish gross
mana-wastage. It just does not distinguish anything above that floor.

Directions, cheapest first:

- Give Efficiency a gradient above par instead of a clamp, so beating par pays.
- Raise the S/A thresholds, or rebalance term weights, once 1a makes Survival real.
- Make `xp_par` genuinely contested rather than incidentally met.

## 3. Difficulty is flat, then a wall

From `tools/simulate.gd`, 40 runs — losses by course:

```
Comprehensive Exam: 26   Necrology 201: 19   Midterm Review: 9
Thesis 301: 1            Pyromancy 201: 1
```

Three graduations in 40 runs, but 11 courses passed per run on average. Everything is
trivial until two specific nodes, and the Rector (80 HP, 3 mana, a deck of *evolved*
cards) is a brick wall rather than a climax. Necrology 201 killing more runs than the
tier-2 gate suggests the Alchemy Master's Decay deck is mis-tiered relative to its
neighbours, not that tier 2 is hard.

This is a curve problem, separate from findings 1 and 2: even with perfect grading,
a flat-then-spike shape is not engaging.

## 4. Chill and Blot scale past a hard ceiling

`Battle.CHILL_REDUCTION = 0.3` and `BLOT_REDUCTION = 0.4` are applied as
`maxf(0.0, 1.0 - REDUCTION * stacks)`, so Chill is fully saturated at **4 stacks**
and Blot at **3**. Both are consumed whole by one card, so surplus stacks are not
banked — they are destroyed.

But the five-level evolution track keeps scaling the number:

| Card | Chill applied | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eternal Winter (L5 Hoarfrost) | 6 | 4 |
| Winter Eternal (L5 Winter Term) | 8 | 4 |

A maxed Frost card spends its evolution budget on a stat that is 100% wasted above
4. The player is being told these cards get stronger, and for two of the five levels
they do not. Either cap the authored amounts at the ceiling, or change the statuses
to persist their surplus.

## 5. No run-to-run variance

`tools/generate_courses.gd` is a static 15-row table: fixed courses, fixed
prerequisite graph, fixed examiners with fixed decks, fixed syllabus cards. The only
thing that varies between runs is the shuffle RNG.

For a roguelike this is structural. Every run presents the same 15 nodes in the same
shape against the same nine examiners. The Bestiary — knowledge carried within a run
— is the only discovery, and once you have played twice you know every weakness
before the run starts, which quietly deletes the Discovery term's purpose.

The cheapest fixes that do not require new content: randomise examiner
weak/warded schools per run (the Bestiary mechanic already exists to rediscover
them), and offer a choice of 2–3 courses at each step rather than revealing all
available nodes.

## Godot practice

These are structural rather than broken. The codebase is unusually well-commented
and the core/UI separation is genuinely good — `scripts/core` is pure `RefCounted`
logic that returns event dictionaries and never touches the scene tree, which is why
3436 headless assertions are possible at all. Keep that.

### 6. The project has one scene and builds every screen in code

`scenes/Main.tscn` is the only `.tscn`. Every screen (`MainMenu`, `CourseCatalog`,
`BattleScreen`, `ReportCard`, `RegistrationScreen`, `GameOver`, `BestiaryScreen`)
is instantiated with `.new()` and assembled in a `_build()` method.

This is the most notable idiom deviation in the project. Costs:

- Nothing is editable or previewable in the Godot editor; layout can only be checked
  by running `tools/shot.sh` and looking at a PNG.
- Layout constants live as magic numbers spread across `_build()` methods
  (`FIGURE_SIZE`, `custom_minimum_size = Vector2(0, 300)`, hand-positioned labels at
  `Vector2(10, CARD_SIZE.y - 112)`).
- The `ui_theme.tres` in project settings can only be partly seen, since much of the
  styling is `add_theme_*_override` calls in code.

It is a defensible choice for a generated project and it does test well. But if
anyone iterates on the UI by hand, `CardView` and `BattleScreen` are the two worth
converting to scenes first — they are the screens with the most hand-placed geometry.

### 7. The hand is destroyed and rebuilt after every single action

`BattleScreen.refresh()` calls `hand_fan.set_hand(...)` unconditionally, and
`HandFan.set_hand()` frees every `CardView` and constructs new ones:

```gdscript
func set_hand(cards: Array) -> void:
	for child in get_children():
		remove_child(child)
		child.queue_free()
```

`refresh()` runs after every card played and every End Turn. So playing a card tears
down and rebuilds the whole hand — each new `CardView._build()` re-runs
`ArtLibrary.texture()`, `ResourceLoader.exists()`, and constructs 5–6 child nodes per
card.

Two consequences:

- It is the reason the game cannot have card animations. There is no persistent node
  to tween — a card that should slide to the discard pile simply blinks out of
  existence. **This is a fun problem, not just a performance one:** the battle has no
  motion, no impact, and no feedback beyond a text log. For a mobile card game that
  is a large part of "works but is not engaging."
- On a phone this is avoidable churn on every tap.

Fix: diff the hand against existing views, reuse the ones that stayed, and tween the
ones that moved.

Also, `remove_child(child)` followed by `child.queue_free()` is redundant —
`queue_free()` removes the node from its parent. It appears in `HandFan.set_hand`
and `BattleScreen._build`; `CardView._build` already does it correctly with a bare
`queue_free()`.

### 8. `ArtLibrary` re-checks the filesystem on every refresh

`ArtLibrary.texture()` calls `has_sprite()` → `ResourceLoader.exists()` on every
call before consulting its cache, and `BattleScreen.refresh()` calls it for the
examiner figure every time anything happens. Only the *procedural fallback* is
cached; the sprite path check is not. Check the cache first.

`static var _cache` is also never cleared and keyed by `key@WxH@school`, so it grows
for the process lifetime. Minor on desktop, worth bounding on mobile.

### 9. Three autoloads, two of which hold duplicate state

Low severity, but worth resolving before more code depends on them:

- `GradeManager` is a pure pass-through to `Grading`'s static methods. Its own
  docstring says it "Exists because the brief names it."
- `DeckManager.deck` is written once (`Main.gd:108`) and read only by tests.
- `GameManager.run` duplicates the `Run` that `Main` already owns, assigned
  alongside it in two places (`Main.gd:75`, `Main.gd:85`) — two references to the
  same object that nothing keeps in sync by construction.

Godot autoloads are best reserved for genuine cross-scene state. Here the pure-logic
classes are already global via `class_name`, so `GradeManager` in particular adds an
indirection with no owner.

Note the cost before acting: `test_autoloads.gd` pins all three (≈40 assertions), so
collapsing them means deleting that suite, not just the scripts. Recommended anyway —
`Main` already is the composition root, and the docstrings admit the redundancy.

### 10. Two objects leak at exit

Both `tools/check.sh` and `tools/simulate.gd` finish with:

```
WARNING: 2 ObjectDB instances were leaked at exit
ERROR: 1 resources still in use at exit
```

Exit code is 0 and the suite passes, so this is not gating anything. The likely cause
is reference cycles among `Resource`s that `RefCounted` cannot collect —
`CardData.evolved_card` chains, `CourseData.prerequisites`, and the deliberately
untyped `Battle._bestiary` (typed as untyped specifically to dodge a cyclic
dependency, per its own comment). Worth confirming with `--verbose` before it grows.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Finding 1a** — make run HP carry into battles, with the F-heal and `hp_start`
   consequences handled in the same change. Smallest diff, largest effect.
2. **Finding 2** — regrade once Survival means something; give Efficiency a gradient.
3. **Finding 1b** — restore the examiner telegraph as a multi-card plan.
4. **Finding 7** — reuse `CardView`s so the battle can have motion.
5. **Findings 3, 4** — rebalance the curve and cap the saturating statuses.
6. **Finding 5** — per-run variance.

1, 2 and 3 are one coherent change to how a fight is won; doing any of them alone
will read as a smaller improvement than it is.

## Reproducing the measurements

```sh
godot --headless --path . --script tools/simulate.gd     -- 40   # difficulty curve
godot --headless --path . --script tools/policy_probe.gd -- 30   # does skill matter
```

Both are seeded (`seed = 1000 + i`), so the numbers in this document are
reproducible. Neither is part of the CI gate.
