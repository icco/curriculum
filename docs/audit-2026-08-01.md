# Curriculum — audit, 2026-08-01

Scope: Godot practice, and why the game "works but is not fun." The test gate is
green — `tools/check.sh`: 25 suites, 3459 checks, 0 failures, including the fixes
below. Nothing here is a broken build; it is all design and structure.

Findings are ordered by how much they cost the player experience. Every gameplay
claim below is measured, not asserted; the method is at the bottom.

**Findings 1a, 2 and 3 have been fixed**, in that order — 1a because it was an
implementation bug, then 2 and 3 together because 1a made the game unwinnable and the
three are coupled. Findings 1b, 4, 5 and the Godot-practice section remain open.
The measurements under each finding are the *pre-fix* numbers that motivated it; each
"After the fix" section records what landed.

---

## The measurement

`tools/policy_probe.gd` (added for this audit) plays 100 headless runs under four
policies and reports what each scores. If a policy that throws away a real resource
scores the same as playing properly, that resource is not a decision.

"Defensive" below means what a card *does* — Block, healing, or retain — not what
school it belongs to. Block is not exclusive to Ward: the Numb the Hall line is Frost
and grants 6–10 Block.

| Policy | Graduated | Courses/run | Mean score | Grades |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **greedy** — play every affordable card | 6/100 | 10.9 | **76.3** | 69 S, 693 A, 315 B, 10 C, 143 F |
| **onecard** — one card per turn, waste the rest of the mana | 0/100 | 1.0 | 50.6 | 4 A, 94 B, 3 C, 200 F |
| **nodefence** — never play Block, healing or retain | **26/100** | 11.5 | 70.4 | 5 S, 553 A, 423 B, 165 C, 106 F |
| **onlydefence** — only ever defend | 0/100 | 0.0 | 42.4 | 200 F |

Per-term averages, out of 25 each:

| Policy | Efficiency | Survival | Learning | Discovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| greedy | 23.3 (maxed in 80% of battles) | 15.8 | 16.6 (maxed 24%) | 20.6 |
| nodefence | 24.8 (maxed 97%) | **16.0** | 12.7 | 16.9 |

Two things fall out of this table, and they are findings 1 and 2.

## 1. Defence is not merely optional — it is a losing move

Never playing a single Block, heal, or retain card across an entire run:

- **ends battles with more HP, not less** — Survival 16.0 vs greedy's 15.8;
- **graduates 4.3× more often** — 26 runs in 100 against greedy's 6.

A fifth of the card pool, and 40% of the starting deck, is worse than useless. You
survive better by never defending, because ending the fight sooner protects more HP
than any Block card does. The correct play is to race, always.

Note the perverse half of this: the *grade* mildly punishes racing (70.4 vs 76.3,
because shorter fights bank less Learning and probe fewer schools), while the *run*
massively rewards it. The scoring system pays you for the style of play that loses
runs. Any regrade has to reckon with that, not just with the thresholds in finding 2.

Three causes, compounding:

### 1a. Run HP is tracked, saved, and never actually used — an implementation bug

Before this branch, `Battle.gd:41` hardcoded the player:

```gdscript
player = Combatant.new("Student", 60, 3)
```

Meanwhile `Run.hp` is written by `Run.record_result`, round-tripped through
`SaveGame`, and displayed — but **never read back into a battle**. Every fight starts
the player at a fresh 60/60 regardless of what the last fight cost.

This is a bug, not a design gap. The spec has the `Run` holding HP as run-scoped
persistent state (§10.5), and §6.1's rule that an F "restores" your HP is only
meaningful if HP otherwise carries. `Run.record_result`'s own
`hp = clampi(hp_end, 1, max_hp)` is written for carryover. Only `Battle` fails to
read it.

The consequence is that there is no attrition, which is the central resource of the
genre. HP is not a currency you spend across a run; it is a per-battle buffer that
resets. That makes healing — Study Break → Tenure, the Ward line's whole payoff —
literally incapable of mattering, and removes any reason to end a fight above 1 HP.

**Two consequences to handle in the same change:**

- `Main.gd` passed `"hp_start": run.max_hp` into `Grading.score`. If HP carries
  and `hp_start` stays `max_hp`, Survival silently becomes "fraction of *max* HP
  remaining", so damage taken three courses ago permanently caps every future grade.
  The fix must pass the battle's *actual* starting HP so Survival keeps measuring
  damage taken *in this battle*.
- `Run.record_result` sets `hp = max_hp` on an F, so once HP carries, failing an exam
  becomes a free full heal — a rest site you are paid to visit. **This is spec'd
  behaviour** (§6.1, deliberately argued for), not a defect, so it is a call for the
  owner rather than something to quietly change. Flagging it because a full heal is
  worth much more once HP is scarce than it was when HP reset anyway.

Fixing 1a is the single highest-leverage change in this document.

#### After the fix

Applied in this branch: `Battle._init` takes an optional `starting_hp` (0 still
meaning "walk in at full", so existing callers are unaffected), `Main` passes
`run.hp`, and both harnesses and `Grading`'s `hp_start` were threaded through to
`battle.player_starting_hp`. Pinned by `tests/test_battle_hp_carryover.gd`.

What it bought, at 100 runs:

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survival, greedy vs nodefence | 15.8 vs **16.0** — defending was *worse* | 13.4 vs **12.7** — defending now helps |
| Graduations, greedy vs nodefence | 6 vs **26** | 6 vs 0 |
| Courses passed per run (greedy) | 10.9 | 6.0 |
| Graduations in 100 (greedy) | 6 | **0** |

Finding 1's pathology is gone: defence is now worth playing, and racing no longer
beats it. The difficulty curve also flattened out much less sharply — losses now
spread across ten different courses instead of piling on two.

**But the game is now considerably too hard**, and this is the honest cost of the
fix: the greedy bot graduated 6 times in 100 before and 0 times after, and 34 of 40
runs end in expulsion. That is not a regression the fix introduced so much as a debt
it revealed — enemy HP, damage and the two-strike rule were all implicitly tuned
against a player who healed to full before every fight. **Finding 3 is now a
prerequisite for shipping, not an improvement.** These bots also play far worse than
a human would, so treat 0/100 as a direction, not a difficulty rating.

### 1b. Nothing telegraphs the examiner's turn — CLOSED, will not change

The spec is unusually emphatic here (§3): "Examiners play from decks too… Its
'intent' is simply the card it drew, shown face-up above it. **That last point is
the load-bearing one.**"

The implementation removed it. `Battle.gd:8-13` documents the removal as deliberate,
and commit `2d73488` ("make examiners a real threat") explains why: examiners were
unloseable, so the examiner was changed to spend its *full* mana each turn, and the
telegraph was deleted in the same change.

The difficulty gain in that commit came from the mana change — one action per turn to
three. Deleting the telegraph contributed no difficulty; it converted every defensive
decision into a coin flip. You cannot decide whether 10 Block is enough when you have
no idea what is landing. Combined with 1a, the rational play is to ignore defence
entirely — which is exactly what the `nodefence` row measures.

**Decision (owner, 2026-08-01): the examiner does not telegraph, and does not
pre-commit its turn. This finding is closed and will not be actioned.** The fight
stays a genuine unknown; the examiner keeps resolving live on its own turn. Spec
section 3 is superseded on this point, and `Battle.gd`'s header comment already
describes the behaviour that ships.

The recommendation above is left in place as the reasoning that was considered, not
as outstanding work — but two things weaken it, and both are worth recording:

1. **It was largely downstream of finding 1a.** Defence was a coin flip *and*
   protected a resource that reset every course. With hit points carrying and
   recovery in, defence now measurably pays without any telegraph at all: greedy
   scores 74.5 against nodefence's 68.9 and takes 272 A grades to its 100. The
   argument that a player cannot make a defensive decision without a telegraph is not
   borne out by the numbers once attrition is real.
2. **Pre-committing is itself a difficulty change**, not just an information one. A
   plan locked at the start of the player's turn cannot react to what the player
   does, which makes examiners predictable and would have needed its own retune to
   hold the 10–20% band.

What the player reads the fight with instead: the log, their own status readout, and
the Bestiary. `BattleScreen`'s header comment already treats those as load-bearing
for exactly this reason, and that is the right place to invest if the fight ever
feels unreadable — a bigger log or clearer status panel, not a telegraph.

### 1c. Block expires before it can be tested cheaply

`Combatant.expire_block()` is correct per spec, but with 1a and 1b in place the
player is asked to pay mana for a resource that (i) protects HP that resets anyway
and (ii) is sized against an unknown threat. All three have to move together.

## 2. Grading saturates: par is a floor, not a target

`Grading.score` clamps three of its four terms at par:

```gdscript
efficiency = 25 * clampf(par_turns / turns_taken, 0.0, 1.0)
learning   = 25 * clampf(xp_banked / xp_par,      0.0, 1.0)
```

Because of the clamp there is **no gradient above par**. Winning in 5 turns against
`par_turns = 5` scores exactly the same 25 as winning in 2. Measured: Efficiency is
maxed in 80% of greedy battles (97% under `nodefence`), averaging 23.3/25 — it is
effectively a constant, not a discriminator. Survival is inflated by finding 1a.

A merely competent win therefore floors around 85 → **A**. Across 1230 battles the
greedy bot took 762 grades of A or better and exactly 10 C's. There is no headroom
for playing well and no meaningful penalty band below it: the grade compresses
almost every outcome into one letter.

This is *the same finding* as "no meaningful decisions", seen from the scoring side.
There is no targeting, no card-ordering leverage and no cost/benefit tension in a
turn, so a bot with no strategy at all is playing close to as well as the game lets
anyone play — and it scores A. That an A-heavy distribution falls out of a policy
with no plan is the evidence that skill does not discriminate.

Sharper still: greedy is not even the *best* of these policies at winning runs.
`nodefence` graduates 26 times in 100 to greedy's 6 while scoring a full 5.9 points
lower. The grade and the run disagree about what good play is.

Worth noting the `onecard` row as the control: throwing away two thirds of your mana
*does* collapse the score (76.3 → 50.6). So the game does punish gross
mana-wastage. It just does not distinguish anything above that floor.

Directions, cheapest first:

- Give Efficiency a gradient above par instead of a clamp, so beating par pays.
- Raise the S/A thresholds, or rebalance term weights, once 1a makes Survival real.
- Make `xp_par` genuinely contested rather than incidentally met.

### After the fix

Both of the first two landed.

`Grading.PAR_TARGET = 1.25` divides the efficiency ratio, so par is now the target
rather than the floor: finishing exactly at par earns 25 / 1.25 = 20, and the last
five points are bought by finishing in four fifths of par. Tier cutoffs moved with
it — A 75 → 78, B 60 → 64, C 40 → 44.

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Efficiency maxed | 80% of battles | 74% |
| Mean Efficiency | 23.3 / 25 | 23.3 / 25 |
| Modal grade | **A** (693 of 1230) | **B** (517 of 1174) |
| S grades | 69 | 23 |
| C grades | 10 | 54 |

The grade is a spread again rather than a formality: B is the ordinary outcome, A is
earned, S is rare, and the C band — which had almost no occupants — is doing work.

`xp_par` was left alone. Learning is maxed in only 14% of battles, so it was already
the term doing the most discriminating; it did not need help.

## 3. Difficulty is flat, then a wall (and, after 1a, too steep everywhere)

Before the 1a fix, from `tools/simulate.gd`, 40 runs — losses by course:

```
Comprehensive Exam: 26   Necrology 201: 19   Midterm Review: 9
Thesis 301: 1            Pyromancy 201: 1
```

Three graduations in 40 runs, but 11 courses passed per run on average. Everything is
trivial until two specific nodes, and the Rector (80 HP, 3 mana, a deck of *evolved*
cards) is a brick wall rather than a climax. Necrology 201 killing more runs than the
tier-2 gate suggests the Alchemy Master's Decay deck is mis-tiered relative to its
neighbours, not that tier 2 is hard.

This is a curve problem, separate from findings 1 and 2: even with perfect grading,
a flat-then-spike shape is not engaging.

After the 1a fix the same 40 runs spread their losses across ten courses rather than
five — the shape improved — but the overall level moved well past fair:

```
Necrology 201: 21   Fieldwork 250: 18   Cryomancy 201: 11   Midterm Review: 9
Proctor's Inspection: 6   Pyromancy 201: 3   Thesis 301: 2   Tutorial 150: 2
Marginalia 201: 1   Applied Wardcraft 301: 1
```

0 graduations in 40, 34 expulsions, 6.1 courses passed per run. Necrology 201 remains
the single worst-tuned node in both measurements, before and after — the Alchemy
Master's Decay deck is mis-tiered relative to its neighbours regardless of attrition.
That is the first thing to look at.

### After the fix

Three changes, in the order they were measured: **recovery** (a mechanism), the
**Efficiency gradient** (finding 2), then a **full roster pass**.

**Recovery.** Passing a course now restores a share of maximum hit points scaled by
the grade earned — 36 / 27 / 18 / 9 percent for S / A / B / C. An F still restores in
full, per spec 6.1. This was structurally required, not a preference: with 15 courses
and 60 hit points and no recovery at all, pure stat tuning would have to cap the
average fight at ~4 damage to be survivable, which makes every fight meaningless.
Scaling it by grade keeps the pillar attached to the scarcest resource. Recovery
alone took courses-passed-per-run from 6.0 to 9.9.

The obvious exploit — an F restores more than any pass, so throw a winnable fight and
bank a full heal — was measured rather than assumed, via a `throwlow` policy in
`policy_probe.gd`. It does not pay, and not narrowly: **2 graduations in 60 against
greedy's 8**, 7.5 courses per run against 10.3, 69.0 mean score against 74.5.

Note the threshold matters and the first attempt at this measured nothing. At a 25 hp
trigger the policy never fired — every course is entered above 25 hp on average — so
it scored within noise of greedy while being greedy. The policy now reports how many
battles it actually diverged on (85 of 559 at a 40 hp trigger); a near-greedy score
from a policy that never engaged is worse than no measurement, because it reads as a
cleared exploit. Spec 6.1's full restore stands, but re-run this if recovery is ever
tuned upward.

**Roster.** All nine examiners were retuned. Two lessons are worth keeping, both
written into `generate_enemies.gd`:

- *Damage density beats hit points.* A fight is lost to what lands per turn, not to
  how long it takes. Cryomancy 201 was a 13.3-turn slog at a 0% loss rate because the
  Drillmaster ran three Chill sources — and Chill cuts the player's damage, so every
  Chill made the fight longer, which produced more Chill.
- *Watch the cost curve against the examiner's mana.* Dropping Alchemy Master's only
  2-cost card to shorten fights made Necrology 201 **worse** — 37% to 53% — because
  at 2 mana an all-1-cost deck plays two cards a turn. Bitter Recall plus Spark is 18
  damage where a single 2-cost card is 11. That cost a full iteration.

Decay's growth was left alone. The spec argues for it explicitly (3.1) and the deck,
not the status, was what made it unfair.

An earlier draft of this document claimed the player has "no counterplay to Decay at
all." **That was wrong**, and `tests/test_decay_counterplay.gd` now proves it: Decay
ticks at the END of the player's turn while Block does not expire until the START of
their next one, so Block gained on the turn Decay lands absorbs it in full. The
control case in that suite shows the same tick costing full hit points unblocked.

The real gap is narrower. Block *mitigates* a tick; nothing *shrinks the stack*.
`Statuses.clear_all()` exists and no battle code calls it, and Decay grows by 2 every
tick whether or not it was blocked — so a long fight eventually outgrows any block
the player can put up. That is arguably correct for a school the spec designs to
"lose a short fight and win a long one", and it is why Alchemy Master must not carry
sustain. Worth a decision rather than a fix.

Measured over 100 runs, greedy bot:

| | Before 1a | After 1a, before this | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Graduated | 6% | 0% | **20%** |
| Courses passed per run | 10.9 | 6.0 | 10.5 |
| Comprehensive Exam loss rate | — | 92% | **57%** |
| Necrology 201 loss rate | — | 35% | 27% |
| Courses at a 0% loss rate | — | 7 of 15 | 5 of 15 |

The target was a 10–20% graduation band — a weak policy should mostly fail a
roguelike — with the greedy-vs-nodefence gap from finding 1 preserved. Both hold:
greedy scores 74.5 against nodefence's 68.9, taking 272 A grades to its 100 and 11 S
to its 1. On raw win count the two are close (8 and 8 at n=60), so the discriminator
is now grade quality rather than survival — which is the intended shape, since grade
buys both recovery and draft allowance.

The curve climbs instead of spiking: tier 1 costs hit points without killing (0–2%),
tier 2 ranges 14–27% with its gate hardest at 38%, and the final sits at 57%.

**The single global difficulty dial is `Grading._RECOVERY`.** It moves every course
at once where a stat change moves one or two, and it is coupled to the tier cutoffs —
tightening those lowers grades, which lowers healing, which is a difficulty change
wearing a scoring change's clothes. Retune the two together and re-measure.

## 4. Chill and Blot scale past a hard ceiling

`Battle.CHILL_REDUCTION = 0.3` and `BLOT_REDUCTION = 0.4` are applied as
`maxf(0.0, 1.0 - REDUCTION * stacks)`, so Chill is fully saturated at **4 stacks**
and Blot at **3**. Both are consumed whole by one card, so surplus stacks are not
banked — they are destroyed.

But the five-level evolution track keeps scaling the number:

| Card | Chill applied | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eternal Winter (L5 Hoarfrost) | 6 | 4 |
| Winter Eternal (L5 Winter Term) | 8 | 4 |

A maxed Frost card spends its evolution budget on a stat that is 100% wasted above
4. The player is being told these cards get stronger, and for two of the five levels
they do not. Either cap the authored amounts at the ceiling, or change the statuses
to persist their surplus.

## 5. No run-to-run variance — PARTLY FIXED

`tools/generate_courses.gd` is a static 15-row table: fixed courses, fixed
prerequisite graph, fixed examiners with fixed decks, fixed syllabus cards. The only
thing that varies between runs is the shuffle RNG.

For a roguelike this is structural. Every run presents the same 15 nodes in the same
shape against the same nine examiners. The Bestiary — knowledge carried within a run
— is the only discovery, and once you have played twice you know every weakness
before the run starts, which quietly deletes the Discovery term's purpose.

The cheapest fixes that do not require new content: randomise examiner
weak/warded schools per run (the Bestiary mechanic already exists to rediscover
them), and offer a choice of 2–3 courses at each step rather than revealing all
available nodes.

### After the fix

**Wards are now rolled per run; weaknesses are not, and that is a measured decision.**

`Faculty` builds one duplicated `EnemyData` per examiner from a per-run seed. The seed
is the only thing saved — generation is pure in (roster, seed, starting schools), so a
continued run faces the faculty it started against rather than a fresh set that would
invalidate everything in its Bestiary.

The obvious version of this finding — roll both schools — is wrong, and badly. Over 120
runs across 12 generated worlds, greedy bot:

| | Graduated | Courses/run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| authored weak + authored ward | 17.5% | 10.4 |
| **authored weak + random ward** | **12.5%** | **9.2** |
| random weak + authored ward | 6.7% | — |
| random weak + random ward | 3.3% | 6.9 |

The authored weaknesses are load-bearing content. They are an implicit tutorial — the
opening courses are weak to Cinder and Ink, exactly the starting deck, and warded
against Frost, which the player owns none of — and the entire roster's hit points and
decks were tuned assuming those matchups. Constraining the roll is not enough:
forcing every examiner to be weak to a school the player already owns still only
reached 7.5%.

Two things this surfaced that are worth keeping:

- **Measure the spread across worlds, never the aggregate.** A generator producing half
  trivial and half impossible worlds averages to a respectable number. `simulate.gd`
  now takes a world count and reports graduation per world; content seed and shuffle
  seed vary independently, so a bad generator is distinguishable from bad luck. The
  first uniform-roll attempt read as "20% → 3%" in aggregate but as **10 of 12 worlds
  at a flat 0%** per world, which is the number that actually diagnosed it.
- **A random ward can resurrect a known degenerate deck.** Warding a mostly-Block
  examiner against the player's own school does not make a hard fight, it makes an
  unbreakable one — Proctor's Inspection measured a 43% loss over 11.1 turns, the exact
  stalemate its roster entry already warned about. `Faculty` therefore refuses that
  combination, measuring "defensive" off the deck rather than a name list.

**Still outstanding:** generative *weaknesses* and generative *decks*. They are one job,
not two — both require retuning the roster to be school-agnostic, so that no deck is
degenerate under any assignment and no fight's difficulty rests on the player's opening
schools. Per-run course choice (offering 2–3 of the available nodes) is separate and
also outstanding; note it needs its own reachability invariant, since `Catalog.validate()`
checks the whole graph and not a sampled subset of it.

## Godot practice

These are structural rather than broken. The codebase is unusually well-commented
and the core/UI separation is genuinely good — `scripts/core` is pure `RefCounted`
logic that returns event dictionaries and never touches the scene tree, which is why
3436 headless assertions are possible at all. Keep that.

### 6. The project has one scene and builds every screen in code

`scenes/Main.tscn` is the only `.tscn`. Every screen (`MainMenu`, `CourseCatalog`,
`BattleScreen`, `ReportCard`, `RegistrationScreen`, `GameOver`, `BestiaryScreen`)
is instantiated with `.new()` and assembled in a `_build()` method.

This is the most notable idiom deviation in the project. Costs:

- Nothing is editable or previewable in the Godot editor; layout can only be checked
  by running `tools/shot.sh` and looking at a PNG.
- Layout constants live as magic numbers spread across `_build()` methods
  (`FIGURE_SIZE`, `custom_minimum_size = Vector2(0, 300)`, hand-positioned labels at
  `Vector2(10, CARD_SIZE.y - 112)`).
- The `ui_theme.tres` in project settings can only be partly seen, since much of the
  styling is `add_theme_*_override` calls in code.

It is a defensible choice for a generated project and it does test well. But if
anyone iterates on the UI by hand, `CardView` and `BattleScreen` are the two worth
converting to scenes first — they are the screens with the most hand-placed geometry.

### 7. The hand is destroyed and rebuilt after every single action — FIXED

`BattleScreen.refresh()` calls `hand_fan.set_hand(...)` unconditionally, and
`HandFan.set_hand()` frees every `CardView` and constructs new ones:

```gdscript
func set_hand(cards: Array) -> void:
	for child in get_children():
		remove_child(child)
		child.queue_free()
```

`refresh()` runs after every card played and every End Turn. So playing a card tears
down and rebuilds the whole hand — each new `CardView._build()` re-runs
`ArtLibrary.texture()`, `ResourceLoader.exists()`, and constructs 5–6 child nodes per
card.

Two consequences:

- It is the reason the game cannot have card animations. There is no persistent node
  to tween — a card that should slide to the discard pile simply blinks out of
  existence. **This is a fun problem, not just a performance one:** the battle has no
  motion, no impact, and no feedback beyond a text log. For a mobile card game that
  is a large part of "works but is not engaging."
- On a phone this is avoidable churn on every tap.

Fix: diff the hand against existing views, reuse the ones that stayed, and tween the
ones that moved.

#### After the fix

`HandFan` now keeps a `CardInstance -> CardView` map. A refresh drops the views for
cards that left the hand, reuses the views of cards still held, builds only what is
new, reorders children to the hand's order, and slides each card to its slot;
newly drawn cards are dealt in from just below theirs. `tests/test_handfan_reuse.gd`
pins it by **object identity** rather than child counts, because a
rebuild-everything implementation passes every count-based assertion identically.

Two things this turned up that the finding above got wrong or missed:

- **`remove_child` before `queue_free` is not redundant, and the original text
  claiming so was wrong.** `queue_free()` is deferred to the end of the frame, so the
  node remains a child until then. `get_child_count()`, the fan's own
  index-by-child-position layout, and the order-sensitive assertions in
  `test_battlescreen.gd` all read that state synchronously. The pairing is
  load-bearing; it is `CardView._build`'s bare `queue_free()` that was the latent bug,
  since a rebuild briefly layered a second full set of children over the first.
- **A reused view has to notice its card evolving.** A `CardInstance` keeps its
  identity while swapping its `CardData` pointer, so a view that reuses on identity
  alone would show the pre-evolution face for the rest of the battle. `CardView.sync()`
  rebuilds only on a data change and otherwise just rewrites the XP line.

### 8. `ArtLibrary` re-checks the filesystem on every refresh — FIXED

`ArtLibrary.texture()` called `has_sprite()` → `ResourceLoader.exists()` on every
call before consulting its cache, and `BattleScreen.refresh()` calls it for the
examiner figure every time anything happens. Only the *procedural fallback* was
cached; the sprite-path check was not, and its answer cannot change while the game is
running.

The cache is now consulted first and holds loaded sprites as well as painted
fallbacks. `clear_cache()` exists for callers that regenerate art on disk. The cache
is bounded by the number of distinct art keys rather than by play length, so it is
left unbounded deliberately.

### 9. Three autoloads, two of which hold duplicate state

Low severity, but worth resolving before more code depends on them:

- `GradeManager` is a pure pass-through to `Grading`'s static methods. Its own
  docstring says it "Exists because the brief names it."
- `DeckManager.deck` is written once (`Main.gd:108`) and read only by tests.
- `GameManager.run` duplicates the `Run` that `Main` already owns, assigned
  alongside it in two places (`Main.gd:75`, `Main.gd:85`) — two references to the
  same object that nothing keeps in sync by construction.

Godot autoloads are best reserved for genuine cross-scene state. Here the pure-logic
classes are already global via `class_name`, so `GradeManager` in particular adds an
indirection with no owner.

Note the cost before acting: `test_autoloads.gd` pins all three (≈40 assertions), so
collapsing them means deleting that suite, not just the scripts. Recommended anyway —
`Main` already is the composition root, and the docstrings admit the redundancy.

### 10. Two objects leak at exit — INVESTIGATED, will not fix

Both `tools/check.sh` and `tools/simulate.gd` finish with:

```
WARNING: 2 ObjectDB instances were leaked at exit
ERROR: 1 resources still in use at exit
```

Exit code is 0 and the suite passes, so this is not gating anything.

`--verbose` names it exactly:

```
Leaked instance: GDScriptNativeClass — Reference count: 1
Leaked instance: GDScript — Reference count: 1
Resource still in use: res://scripts/data/CourseData.gd (GDScript)
```

It is `CourseData`, and the cause is its `@export var prerequisites: Array[CourseData]`
— a typed array of the script's own class. The script ends up holding a reference to
its own type, which is a cycle GDScript's refcounting cannot break at process exit.
`Battle._bestiary` and the `CardData.evolved_card` chain, both suspected above, are
not involved.

**It does not grow.** Measured at 1 run, at 40 runs, and across the whole 3463-check
suite, the count is 2 every time — it is a fixed, script-level artifact rather than a
per-object leak, and nothing accumulates during play.

Closing this rather than fixing it. The only ways to break the cycle are to drop the
self-referential typed export — storing prerequisites as course *names* and resolving
them through `ContentLibrary`, or weakening the type to `Array[Resource]` — and both
trade a correct, statically-typed content graph (the thing `Catalog.validate()`
relies on) for two objects at exit that cost nothing while the game runs. Not a good
trade. Recorded here so the next person does not re-investigate it.
